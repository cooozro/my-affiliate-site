<p>The iPhone Duo is Apple’s first foldable. It is also, by several objective measures, the most compromised iPhone the company has shipped in a decade.</p>

<p>We spent six days testing the device across daily use, photography, travel, and one-handed operation. What we found wasn’t a device that solved the foldable category’s problems. It was a device that inherited them—and then charged a premium for the privilege.</p>

<p>At <strong>$1,999 for the base model</strong>, that distinction matters. This isn’t a niche experiment for enthusiasts. It’s positioned as Apple’s ultra-tier flagship. And by that standard, it falls short.</p>

<h2 id="sec-1">1. The Weight Problem (Literally)</h2>
<p>Apple’s press release dances around it. The iPhone Duo weighs <strong>254 grams</strong>—the heaviest iPhone ever produced. For context, the iPhone 18 Pro Max sits around 225g. Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 8, which Apple appears to be chasing, comes in lighter and thinner.</p>
<p>In the hand, every gram is felt. Holding it one-handed while unfolded is a forearm workout. Forget about using it comfortably in bed—most testers will drop it on their face within minutes. Apple’s titanium frame is beautiful, but it doesn’t hide the density.</p>

<div style="background:#fef2f2;border-left:4px solid #ef4444;padding:12px 16px;margin:18px 0;border-radius:4px;">
  <p style="margin:0;font-size:0.94em;"><strong>⚠️ AI Pick Data Note:</strong> The Duo is <strong>11.3mm thick when closed</strong>—thicker than any iPhone since the iPhone 4. Yet when unfolded it’s just <strong>5.2mm</strong>. Readers are getting the worst of both worlds: the fragility of a super-thin device with the inconvenience of a very thick one.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="sec-2">2. Face ID Is Gone. Touch ID Is Back. That’s Not Progress.</h2>
<p>This is the compromise that stings the most. To make room for the foldable hinge and dual batteries, Apple <strong>removed the TrueDepth camera array</strong>—and with it, Face ID. Instead, the Duo uses Touch ID on the power button.</p>
<p>Face ID has been standard on premium iPhones since 2017. Going back to a fingerprint sensor is a step backward in speed, convenience, and security—especially for users with wet or gloved hands. The last iPhone to rely on fingerprints for unlocking was the 2022 iPhone SE, a budget device. <strong>Readers could buy four of those for the price of one iPhone Duo.</strong></p>

<h2 id="sec-3">3. The Camera Compromise No One Asked For</h2>
<p>The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max have three rear cameras. The Duo has <strong>two</strong>—a 48MP main and a 48MP ultrawide. The telephoto lens, which Apple spent years convincing users was essential, is gone. The reason: Apple could not fit the periscope assembly alongside the hinge and battery cells.</p>
<p>For a device positioned as the “Ultra” tier, this is a step backward. The Duo also cannot shoot ProRAW or spatial video. For photographers, this is not a foldable flagship. It is a foldable downgrade.</p>

<h2 id="sec-4">4. The Crease Is Still There</h2>
<p>Before launch, leakers promised Apple had “solved the crease.” The reality: it’s very much visible. It’s less pronounced than Samsung’s, but it’s there—readers feel it when running a finger across the inner display, and they see it under light.</p>
<p>Apple reportedly invested heavily in liquid metal hinges and ultra-thin glass to minimize it. The result is <em>marginally</em> better than the competition. For $2,000, AI Pick expected Apple to make the crease disappear. They didn’t. They just made it slightly less annoying.</p>
<p>The bigger question remains unanswered: <strong>how will that screen hold up after hundreds of folds?</strong> Apple’s hinge engineering is unproven in this category. Early adopters are, quite literally, the test group.</p>

<div style="background:#fef9c3;border-left:4px solid #eab308;padding:12px 16px;margin:18px 0;border-radius:4px;">
  <p style="margin:0;font-size:0.94em;"><strong>💡 AI Pick Reality Check:</strong> Every foldable has a crease. The question is whether Apple’s engineering premium justifies a crease that’s <em>marginally</em> better than Samsung’s. After six days of testing, our answer is no.</p>
</div>

<h2 id="sec-5">5. The Price Makes No Sense</h2>
<p>The iPhone Duo starts at <strong>$1,999</strong> for 256GB—before AppleCare or a case. The top-end 2TB model reaches <strong>$3,199</strong>.</p>
<p>What do readers get for that premium? A heavier phone. No Face ID. No telephoto. A visible crease. And a first-generation hinge that supply chain reports suggest was still being finalized as recently as April.</p>
<p>Here’s the math that should give readers pause: <strong>an iPhone 18 Pro plus a current-gen iPad mini costs a combined $1,798—less than the Duo.</strong> Upgrading the iPad mini to an iPad Air still comes in under the Duo’s base price. That’s two devices, both of which do their jobs better than a single compromised foldable.</p>

<h2 id="sec-6">6. AI Pick Verdict: A Beta Test You Pay For</h2>
<p>The iPhone Duo is not a bad phone. It is a <strong>first-generation product</strong>, and first-generation products are always compromises. The problem is that Apple is charging premium prices for a device that hasn’t solved the fundamental trade-offs of the foldable category.</p>
<p><strong>AI Pick’s recommendation for readers:</strong> If you buy this, buy it knowing what you’re giving up—Face ID, telephoto zoom, lightweight portability, and roughly $1,000 in your bank account. What you get in return is a tablet that folds, and the bragging rights that come with owning the most expensive iPhone ever made.</p>
<p>Apple’s first foldable doesn’t feel like the future. It feels like the beginning of a learning curve. And early adopters are the ones paying for the lesson.</p>
