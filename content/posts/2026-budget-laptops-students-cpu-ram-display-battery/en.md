## What This Comparison Settles for Student Buyers

Choosing a budget laptop for college usually comes down to a handful of trade-offs: how much CPU you actually need for note-taking and research, whether you can upgrade RAM later, whether the display is bright enough for a sunlit library, and whether the battery survives a full day of classes. This head-to-head looks at two current 2026 models that sit in the same price band but take different approaches to those four questions.

We are comparing the **Acer Aspire 5 (A515-59, Ryzen 5 7530U)** and the **Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 (16IRL9, Core i5-1335U)**. Both are widely listed at retail in the $550–$650 range, and both are popular picks in student-focused forums. Our editorial read is based on public spec sheets, listed retail prices, and open reviews — not a hands-on lab test. The full cross-checking methodology lives on our /about page.

If you are also weighing a Chromebook or a tablet for lighter work, our 2026 budget tablets for reading and video scenario guide covers that alternative aisle.

## 1. Acer Aspire 5 in Daily Use

The Acer Aspire 5 with the Ryzen 5 7530U is the traditionalist's choice: a roomy 15.6-inch chassis with a full set of ports and, crucially, two SODIMM slots. That means the 16GB of DDR4 can be swapped to 32GB later, which is a real advantage if you start running virtual machines for a computer science course or keep dozens of browser tabs open while writing.

In daily use, the Ryzen 5 handles Office, Google Docs, Zoom, and light photo editing without breaking a sweat. The 6-core/12-thread design is efficient for multitasking, and the integrated Radeon graphics can even handle light gaming at low settings, though that is not the focus here. The keyboard is comfortable for long typing sessions, and the Ethernet port is a quiet lifesaver in dorm rooms with flaky Wi-Fi.

**Weaknesses:** The display tops out at 250 nits, which is dim under bright fluorescent lights or when you work near a window. The chassis is a bit bulky, and the 50Wh battery is the smaller of the two, so you will likely need to charge once during a long day. The fan can also become audible under sustained load.

**Editorial read:** If you value future-proofing and repairability over thinness, the Aspire 5 is the more sensible long-term investment. The RAM upgrade path alone can extend its useful life by two years.

## 2. Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 in Daily Use

The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 with the Core i5-1335U is the polished alternative: a lighter, slimmer 16-inch laptop with a brighter 300-nit display and a slightly larger battery. The 10-core Intel chip (2 performance + 8 efficiency cores) is snappy for bursty tasks like opening apps and switching between tabs, and the LPDDR5 memory is faster than the Acer's DDR4, which helps in memory-heavy workloads.

For a student who sits in lecture halls with large windows or studies in a coffee shop with glare, the 300-nit panel is noticeably more legible. The extra 6.6Wh of battery capacity, combined with the efficient efficiency cores, often translates to an extra hour of real-world use — enough to get through a full day of classes without a charger.

**Weaknesses:** The RAM is soldered, so you cannot upgrade beyond 16GB. That is a hard ceiling if your major requires heavier simulation or data analysis software later. The single M.2 slot also means storage upgrades require replacing the existing drive, and there is no Ethernet port, so you rely on Wi-Fi or a USB adapter.

**Editorial read:** The IdeaPad Slim 5 is the better "out of the box" experience for the average liberal arts or business student who values portability and screen quality over tinkering. The soldered RAM is the main reason to pause if you see yourself needing more memory before graduation.

## Scenario Winners: Who Takes Each Class? 

**Best for computer science and engineering majors:** Acer Aspire 5. The upgradeable RAM and extra 2.5-inch drive bay let you expand to 32GB and add a second SSD for VMs or large datasets, which the IdeaPad cannot match. The Ethernet port is also a plus for stable lab connections.

**Best for long library sessions and outdoor study:** Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5. The 300-nit display and larger battery give it a clear edge in bright environments and all-day endurance. If you never plan to open the case, this is the easier daily companion.

**Best for budget flexibility:** Acer Aspire 5. Its slightly lower street price and upgrade path mean you can buy a cheaper configuration now and add RAM later, spreading the total cost of ownership over three years.

**Best for portability:** Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5. It is a bit lighter and slimmer, making it easier to carry across campus. The difference is small but noticeable in a backpack.

**Review concern:** The Acer's 250-nit display is a genuine weak point for bright rooms, and the IdeaPad's soldered RAM is a permanent limitation. Neither is a dealbreaker for every student, but they are the two issues we would flag in a store demo.

## FAQ

### How much RAM do I really need for college?

For note-taking, web research, and document writing, 8GB is the bare minimum, but 16GB is the sweet spot in 2026. It lets you keep dozens of browser tabs open, use Zoom alongside a word processor, and run light photo or video editing without slowdowns. If you are in a technical major, 16GB is the starting point, and having an upgrade path to 32GB is a smart safety net.

### Is a 250-nit display good enough for a student?

It is acceptable for indoor use away from direct light, but it can feel dim in bright lecture halls or near windows. A 300-nit panel, like the one on the IdeaPad, is noticeably more comfortable in those conditions. If you often study in sunny spots, prioritize a brighter display over a slightly faster CPU.

### Can I upgrade the RAM in these laptops later?

The Acer Aspire 5 has two SODIMM slots, so you can replace the 16GB with 32GB later. The Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5 has soldered LPDDR5, so 16GB is the maximum you will ever have. Check the service manual for your specific model before buying if upgradeability matters to you.

### How long should the battery last for a full day of classes?

Aim for at least 8 hours of real-world use. Both laptops claim 10–11 hours, but actual battery life depends on screen brightness, Wi-Fi, and apps. The IdeaPad's larger battery and efficient cores typically give it a slight edge in real-world endurance. Carrying a small USB-C charger is still a good idea for long days.

### Should I pay extra for a dedicated graphics card?

For most students, no. Integrated graphics (like the Radeon in the Ryzen 5 or the Intel Iris Xe) handle video playback, light photo editing, and casual gaming. Only if you are in a creative or engineering major that requires 3D rendering or CAD should you consider a budget laptop with a discrete GPU, which usually costs $200–$300 more and reduces battery life.

## Final Verdict

If we had to pick one for the widest range of students, the **Acer Aspire 5** edges out the IdeaPad because of its upgradeable RAM and lower price. The 250-nit display is a compromise, but the ability to add 32GB of RAM later makes it a better three-year investment, especially for technical majors. The **Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 5** is the better choice for students who prioritize screen brightness, portability, and all-day battery over tinkering — and who are confident 16GB will be enough.

**Who should skip the Acer:** If you spend most of your study time in bright, sunlit spaces and hate carrying a charger, the dimmer display and smaller battery will frustrate you.

**Who should skip the Lenovo:** If you are entering a computer science or engineering program and foresee needing more than 16GB of RAM, the soldered memory will force you to buy a new laptop sooner.

Cross-check the specs on the manufacturer's site before you buy, and remember that RAM upgradeability often matters more than a small clock-speed difference.
