## Why the Anker 737 keeps showing up in 2026 power-bank conversations

![Anker 737 PowerBank (PowerCore 24K) in a real-world lifestyle setting (stock photo illustration)](/images/posts/2026-power-banks-anker-737-review/body-1-c95bb6.jpg)

*Staged category example image (not a photo of the actual product). Photo by aiiapromogifts / Pixabay*

The Anker 737 PowerBank (PowerCore 24K) keeps returning to the top of high-wattage charging conversations because it sits at the intersection of two practical problems: laptops that want real power on the go, and airline rules that cap what you can carry. Many midrange power banks still stall around 65W, which is enough for a phone but often not enough to keep a laptop from draining under load. The 737 pairs a 140W USB-C PD 3.1 output with 86.4Wh of capacity—the largest class you can typically bring through airport security without special airline approval.

We cross-checked Anker’s published product sheet, flight-safety guidance, and open owner reports before writing this review. **Editorial read:** the 737 is not a pocket battery. It is a desk-to-backpack power hub that makes the most sense when a 65W-class pack would stall on a MacBook Pro, a USB-C tablet, or a simultaneous phone-plus-laptop routine. If your commute is short, it may be overkill. If your workday includes a train, plane, or heat-season outage, it earns its spot.

## Design and everyday carry: the weight nobody talks about

The 737 is dense in a way that online photos do not fully communicate. At about 630 grams, it is roughly the weight of a small hardcover book or a chunky power drill battery. That is fine in a backpack or messenger bag, but awkward in a jacket pocket or when holding a phone one-handed for a long video call. The casing feels sturdy, with a grippy texture that prevents it from sliding across a desk.

The digital smart display is genuinely useful: it shows remaining percentage, active output wattage, and temperature in real time, so you can see whether a port is actually delivering 27W, 65W, or 140W. **Review concern:** the readout can make people anxious about temperature spikes. Some owner reports mention that the case gets warm around the display during sustained 100W+ charging. That is expected for GaN power delivery, but it is not a device you want to bury under clothes while it is working hard.

## Core performance: the 140W promise under real device loads

In practice, the USB-C1 port can hold a 16-inch MacBook Pro at a meaningful charge rate while the laptop is awake and under load—something a 65W bank often cannot do. For phone users, USB-C2 and USB-A can top up a Galaxy S25 Ultra, iPhone 17 Pro, or iPad Pro at their native fast-charge ceilings. The biggest real-world limitation is simultaneous output: the 737 cannot simultaneously deliver maximum power from every port. When both USB-C ports are in use, total output is distributed intelligently, but it is not additive.

Recharging the 737 itself is a separate cost conversation. To reach the fastest refill, you need a PD 3.1 wall charger capable of 140W and a cable rated for at least 140W—neither is included in the main box beyond a basic USB-C cable. With the right adapter, a full refill can happen in roughly an hour to an hour and a half; with a standard 30W phone charger, it can take most of a day. **Analysis takeaway:** the headline speed is real, but it is gated by accessories you may already need to buy.

That leads directly to total cost of ownership. The 737 typically lists around $149.99 before sales ({{krw:149.99}}). If you do not already own a 140W GaN wall adapter, budget for a compatible one—usually around $69.99 to $89.99 depending on brand ({{krw:69.99}}). Add a 240W-rated USB-C cable, often around $15.99 ({{krw:15.99}}), and the practical first-day cost is closer to $235–255 before tax. Over a three-year horizon, that is still reasonable if you use the 737 twice a week, but it is not the same as buying a pocket phone charger on impulse.

## Who should buy / who should skip

### Who should buy

- **Laptop-first travelers** who need to keep a USB-C laptop alive on long flights, train rides, or all-day event floors.
- **Multi-device commuters** who regularly charge a phone, earbuds, and tablet from one central battery.
- **People preparing for outages** who want a high-capacity buffer during summer storms or blackouts.

### Who should skip

- **Phone-only minimalists** who rarely leave a wall outlet for more than a few hours. A slim 5,000–10,000 mAh pocket pack will cost less and weigh half as much.
- **Buyers without a high-wattage charger or cable** who do not want to spend extra just to unlock the headline refill speed.
- **Local-service-sensitive buyers** who need easier warranty walk-in support or prefer a product sold through a retailer with fast local return options. Check the seller’s warranty path before paying
- **Anyone expecting silent, cool operation** during sustained 100W+ output. The 737 handles heat safely, but the case gets noticeably warm by design.

The tradeoff is capacity and headroom: the Blade 2 is closer to 74Wh and peaks at 100W, so a 16-inch laptop under heavy load may consume battery faster than the bank can replenish it.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to charge the Anker 737 itself?

With a 140W PD 3.1 wall charger and a properly rated cable, you can expect a full refill in roughly 60 to 90 minutes. With a standard 30W phone charger, it may take 8 to 12 hours. The fastest recharging route is USB-C1, the same port that delivers the highest output to your devices.

### Can it charge a MacBook Pro and an iPhone at the same time?

Yes. USB-C1 can feed a MacBook Pro at high wattage while USB-C2 handles an iPhone fast charge. The total output is shared, so you will not get 140W and 100W simultaneously, but the power bank does a good job of prioritizing the laptop without starving the phone.

### Is the 86.4Wh capacity allowed on airplanes?

For most commercial flights, battery packs up to 100Wh are allowed in carry-on baggage without special airline approval. The 737’s 86.4Wh rating sits below that ceiling, so it is generally carry-on legal. Always check your specific airline before flying because rules can vary by carrier and destination.

### Does it support pass-through charging?

Yes, the 737 supports pass-through, meaning it can be recharged while it charges a connected device. The output may be reduced while the battery is refilling, and the case will run warmer. For overnight travel, this can be useful if you only have one wall outlet.

### What charger do I need to get the full 140W speed?

You need a USB-C wall charger that supports PD 3.1 up to 140W and a USB-C cable rated for at least 140W. Many standard phone chargers top out at 65W or lower, which will still work but will refill the 737 much more slowly.

### Is the smart display accurate?

Owner reports and cross-checks generally describe the display as close enough for real-world use, but not laboratory-precise. The percentage may drift a point or two under heavy load, and the temperature readout reflects internal sensors rather than the exact outer case temperature. Treat it as a useful indicator, not a calibrated instrument.

## Final Verdict
The Anker 737 PowerBank (PowerCore 24K) remains a buy for people who genuinely carry a laptop away from a wall outlet and want the maximum carry-on legal battery capacity. Skip it if your charging needs stop at a phone, or if you are not ready to buy a 140W charger and rated cable to make the headline speeds work. Wait if budget is the deciding factor—the price is stable enough that waiting for a seasonal sale is reasonable, but do not choose a 65W pack as a substitute for laptop protection.
