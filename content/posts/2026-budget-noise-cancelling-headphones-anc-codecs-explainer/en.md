## The question this article answers

"Can a $70 pair of noise-cancelling headphones actually silence a plane cabin?" That's the question we hear most from readers planning summer trips. The short answer: yes, but the depth of cancellation varies wildly depending on how you measure it and what you're listening to.

This explainer is not a ranking. It's a decoder ring for the specs that matter — ANC depth in decibels, codec support (AAC, LDAC, aptX), and the comfort factors that determine whether you'll actually wear them for a six-hour flight. We've cross-checked public spec sheets and open reviews from major brands to give you a realistic picture of what budget models deliver in 2026.

## How the spec actually works

### ANC depth: the decibel reality

Noise cancellation is measured in decibels (dB) of reduction. A claim of "up to 35 dB" sounds impressive, but that number is usually achieved only in a narrow frequency band — typically around 200–300 Hz, where engine rumble lives. Real-world effectiveness varies by frequency: low-frequency noise (aircraft hum, bus engines) is easier to cancel than mid-range chatter (conversations, keyboard clacks) or high-pitched sounds (baby cries, train brakes).

Budget headphones often quote a single "up to" figure that reflects the best-case scenario. For example, the Soundcore Life Q30 lists "up to 40 dB hybrid active noise cancellation," but open reviews consistently show about 20–25 dB of real attenuation across the audible spectrum. That's still meaningful — it turns a loud cabin into a muffled one — but it's not silence.

### Codec support: what your phone actually sends

Codecs determine how audio is transmitted wirelessly. The most common on budget models:

- **AAC**: default on iPhones, good quality, low latency.
- **SBC**: the baseline Bluetooth codec, universally supported but lower quality.
- **aptX / aptX Adaptive**: common on Android, better bitrate than SBC, but not supported by iPhones.
- **LDAC**: Sony's high-resolution codec, up to 990 kbps, but only on Android and rarely found on budget headphones.

Here's the catch: if you use an iPhone, aptX and LDAC are irrelevant — your phone will fall back to AAC. So a budget headphone that supports LDAC (like the Anker Soundcore Space One) only benefits Android users. For iPhone users, AAC quality is the ceiling, and most budget models handle that fine.

### Comfort: the spec that isn't on the box

Comfort is subjective, but there are measurable factors: clamping force (how tightly the headband presses), ear cup depth (whether your ears touch the driver), and weight. Budget models often cut corners here — stiff headbands, shallow ear cups, or pleather that traps heat. For summer travel, breathable fabric ear pads and a lightweight frame (under 250 g) matter more than a few extra dB of ANC.

## Easy misreads

### "Up to 40 dB" means total silence

No. The "up to" figure is a marketing peak. In practice, you'll still hear voices, alarms, and high-pitched noises. Budget ANC is about reducing fatigue, not creating a vacuum.

### "LDAC support" makes any headphone sound great

LDAC only helps if your source (phone) and your ears can perceive the difference. On a noisy plane, background noise masks subtle audio detail. Also, LDAC at 990 kbps drains battery faster — some budget models drop to lower bitrates automatically.

### "Battery 70 hours" means you can forget charging

That figure is usually with ANC off. With ANC on, battery life often drops by 30–40%. The JBL Tune 770NC claims 70 hours with ANC off and 44 hours with ANC on — still excellent, but check the fine print.

### "Comfortable for all-day wear" is a universal truth

Comfort is personal. If you wear glasses, thick ear pads can cause pressure. If you have larger ears, shallow cups will touch. Always try before you buy, or buy from a retailer with a good return policy.

## FAQ

### How much noise cancellation do I really need for a flight?

For a typical flight, 20–25 dB of real ANC is enough to reduce engine roar to a distant hum. If you're sensitive to noise or fly frequently, aim for models that measure closer to 30 dB, like the Soundcore Space One. Remember, ANC won't eliminate crying babies or loud conversations — those are mid-range frequencies.

### Do budget headphones support the same codecs as premium ones?

Most budget models support AAC and SBC, which are the basics. Some, like the Soundcore Space One, add LDAC for Android users. You won't find aptX Adaptive on many budget models, but that's rarely a dealbreaker for casual listening.

### Is it worth spending extra for LDAC?

If you're an Android user who listens to high-resolution audio files, LDAC can improve clarity. But on a plane, with ambient noise, the difference is subtle. For most people, AAC or SBC is perfectly fine. Spend the extra money on better comfort or battery life instead.

### What's the most important comfort factor for travel?

Weight and ear pad material. Under 250 g is ideal for long flights. Fabric ear pads breathe better in summer, while pleather can make your ears sweaty. Also, check that the headband doesn't put too much pressure on the top of your head.

### How do I know if a headphone's ANC is good without trying it?

Look for independent measurements in reviews, not just the marketing "up to" figure. Sites like Rtings or YouTube reviewers often measure real attenuation. Also, check if the headphones have multiple ANC modes — adjustable modes usually indicate more sophisticated processing.

## Key takeaways

- **Editorial read**: Budget ANC headphones under $100 have improved significantly. The Anker Soundcore Space One offers the best balance of ANC depth, codec support (LDAC for Android), and comfort for its price.
- **Review concern**: Battery life claims are often with ANC off. Always check the ANC-on figure. Also, comfort is subjective — try before you buy.
- **Analysis takeaway**: For iPhone users, codec support is less critical; AAC is universal. For Android users, LDAC is a nice bonus but not essential.
- **Who should skip**: If you need to block out conversations in an open office, budget ANC won't deliver. Consider closed-back passive isolation or in-ear monitors. Also, if you have a large head or wear thick glasses, test comfort first.
- **Total cost of ownership**: Over three years, a $100 pair of headphones is cheaper than two $50 pairs that break. Look for models with replaceable ear pads and a good warranty. Budget doesn't mean disposable.
