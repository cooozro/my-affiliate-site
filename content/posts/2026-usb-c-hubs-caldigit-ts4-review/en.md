## Who this review is for

If your laptop has a Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 port and you are tired of plugging and unplugging a tangle of cables every time you sit down, this review is for you. The CalDigit TS4 is not a cheap accessory, so before you spend over $300, it helps to know exactly what it does and what it does not solve.

This is also for people who work from a single desk most of the week — not for someone who travels daily and needs the lightest possible hub. If you run an external monitor, keep a webcam and microphone plugged in, and want one cable to connect everything, the TS4 is the kind of product that quietly changes your routine.

## A day with the device

A typical day starts with one Thunderbolt cable from the TS4 to a laptop. That single connection brings up a 4K monitor, charges the laptop, and wakes the external SSD. There is no scramble to find the right adapter before a video call.

The front of the dock is where the everyday ports live: a 3.5mm headphone jack, an SD card slot, and two USB-C ports. For a photographer or video editor, the SD slot is a small but meaningful convenience — no dongle needed after a shoot. The rear panel holds the heavy-duty connections: DisplayPort, two Thunderbolt 4 downstream ports, and multiple USB-A ports.

One thing that stands out during the day is the 98W power delivery. A 16-inch MacBook Pro stays topped up even under load, which is rare among docks in this class. By the evening, the desk looks almost the same as it did in the morning — nothing was unplugged, nothing was lost.

Thunderbolt 4 certification means the TS4 guarantees 40Gbps bandwidth on its TB4 ports, but the real value is in the mix of speeds across the other ports. The 2.5GbE port is a quiet upgrade over older docks — if you move large files to a NAS, it cuts transfer time noticeably.

The dock also supports two external displays on a compatible host, which is useful for MacBook users who want a second monitor without a DisplayLink driver. That is a genuine compatibility advantage over many cheaper hubs.

## Strengths and weaknesses

**Strengths**

The build quality is the first thing you notice. The aluminum chassis feels solid and acts as a heatsink, so the dock stays cool even when several ports are active. The 98W power delivery is the headline spec, but the port selection is what makes it a desk anchor rather than a travel dongle.

The SD and microSD slots are fast enough for RAW photo transfer, and the front USB-C port makes quick phone backups painless. For a creative professional, the TS4 replaces three separate adapters with one device.

**Weaknesses**

The price is the obvious barrier. At around $360 MSRP, the TS4 costs more than many budget laptops. The included Thunderbolt cable is short, so you may need to buy a longer one depending on your desk layout. Also, the dock is not bus-powered — it needs its own power brick, which adds weight if you ever move it.

Another limitation: the TS4 does not support Thunderbolt 3 on older Intel Macs as reliably as on newer machines. If your laptop is more than four years old, check your host's TB4 or USB4 support before buying.

## One rival, briefly

The Anker 778 Thunderbolt Dock is the most common alternative. It offers 12 ports, 85W charging, and a smaller footprint at a lower price, usually around $250. For a laptop with a 14-inch screen and lighter power needs, the Anker 778 is a sensible choice.

## FAQ

### Can the CalDigit TS4 charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at full speed?

Yes, the TS4 delivers up to 98W to the host, which is enough to charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro under moderate load. Under heavy sustained load, the laptop may draw slightly more than the dock provides, but in normal daily use the battery stays level or increases.

### Does the TS4 work with Windows laptops?

It works with any laptop that has a Thunderbolt 4 or USB4 port. Windows machines with USB4 support will see the full 40Gbps bandwidth, while older Thunderbolt 3 hosts may have limited display support. Check your laptop's port specification before buying.

### Can I connect two 4K monitors to the TS4?

On a compatible host, yes. The TS4 supports dual 4K@60Hz displays through a combination of DisplayPort and Thunderbolt outputs. Some Windows laptops with strict display routing may require specific port combinations, so test with your setup.

### Is the CalDigit TS4 worth it for a student?

For a student with a single laptop and no external monitor, the TS4 is overkill. A compact USB-C hub for under $50 would cover the basics. The TS4 makes sense when you have a permanent desk with multiple peripherals and a large display.

### How long does the CalDigit TS4 last?

CalDigit has a reputation for long product cycles and reliable firmware updates. The TS4 has been on the market for several years and remains a top recommendation, which suggests it will stay relevant for many more. The aluminum build also holds up well over time.

## Buy / wait / skip

**Buy if** you have a Thunderbolt 4 laptop, a permanent desk, and a need for 18 ports without a second hub. The 98W charging and 2.5GbE are features you will appreciate every day.

**Wait if** your laptop is older than four years or you are unsure whether it supports Thunderbolt 4. Also wait if you are about to switch laptops — a TB4 dock is a long-term investment, so match it to your next machine.

**Skip if** you travel constantly, use only a mouse and a single monitor, or want a bus-powered hub for occasional use. The TS4 is a desk component, not a travel accessory.

**Review concern:** the price and the short included cable are the two things we would flag. Budget for a longer Thunderbolt cable if your desk layout demands it, and confirm your host's TB4 support to avoid a disappointing first connection.
