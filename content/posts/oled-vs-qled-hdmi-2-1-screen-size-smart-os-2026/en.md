## Why this TV decision is harder than it looks

For many buyers, OLED vs QLED is no longer a simple contrast ratio debate. Panel brightness, room reflections, HDMI 2.1 port count, and smart OS habits now shape the purchase as much as black levels do. This guide compares two current retail models that represent the two mainstream paths in {{today}}: the LG OLED evo C5 and the Samsung Neo QLED QN90F, with a note on Sony Bravia 8 for Google TV shoppers. We cross-checked manufacturer spec sheets, public retail listings, and open owner reviews; this is an editorial decision guide, not a lab measurement.

In fall, many households start planning a living room upgrade before holiday movie season and next-generation console gaming. A well-chosen TV should last five to seven years, so the goal is matching panel strengths to the room you actually watch in, not the showroom.

## LG OLED evo C5: dark-room depth and four HDMI 2.1 ports

### Strengths
The C5's per-pixel light control gives it an immediate advantage in a light-controlled room. Letterbox bars stay truly black, shadow detail in sci-fi or horror scenes remains visible, and off-angle viewing is more consistent than typical VA-based QLED panels. The current generation adds a brighter OLED evo panel, but its real selling point for gamers is the full set of HDMI 2.1 features across four inputs.

### Weaknesses
Bright living rooms are the weak spot. A sunny bay window can wash out dark scenes, and aggressive ambient light reduces the perceived contrast benefit. OLED also retains a small risk of image retention if you leave a fixed news ticker or game HUD on for many hours daily, though current pixel-refresh and screen-shift routines make this less common than older models.

### Our read
If you are buying a TV for a dedicated media room, a bedroom with curtains, or a desk setup where you connect a PC and current-gen console, the C5 is the stronger panel. You should still budget for a bias light or blackout shades rather than expecting HDR highlights to overpower midday glare.

### Verdict
The C5 wins on black-level authority and connection flexibility. It is the better pick when room light is controllable and dark-room contrast matters more than raw brightness.

## Samsung Neo QLED QN90F: bright-room muscle

### Strengths
The QN90F uses mini-LED backlighting with dense local dimming, which pushes full-screen and specular brightness far beyond typical OLED. Sports, reality TV, news tickers, and HDR demos in a daylight living room hold up with less reflection fighting. It carries a robust gaming mode and supports FreeSync Premium Pro, making it a practical family TV that also handles consoles well.

### Weaknesses
The trade-off is blooming around bright objects on dark backgrounds. It is not a flaw you will notice on a hockey broadcast, but it is visible in letterboxed films with subtitles or starfields. Off-angle color and contrast also shift more than OLED, so a wide sectional may put end seats at a slight disadvantage.

### Our read
Neo QLED is the rational living-room choice. If your TV faces a window or you watch a lot of daytime content, the QN90F's brightness advantage outweighs black-depth purity. For mixed family viewing, it also avoids the faint but persistent worry some OLED owners have about static logos.

### Verdict
The QN90F wins in bright, multi-seat, mixed-content rooms. It is a strong all-rounder when ambient light is the main variable.

If you want a third path, Sony Bravia 8 pairs an OLED panel with Google TV and Sony's processing. It tends to cost more than the LG, but it is worth checking if you prefer Google's app row and voice assistant ecosystem over webOS or Tizen.

## FAQ

### Is OLED really worth the extra money over QLED in 2026?

For dark-room movie lovers and gamers, yes. The extra contrast is not a marketing trick; it changes how shadows, neon signs, and letterbox bars look. If your room is bright and you mostly watch cable news or daytime sports, a good Neo QLED often delivers more real-world impact for less money. The right question is not which panel is technically better, but which room you are putting it in.

### What screen size should I choose for a 3-meter viewing distance?

At roughly 3 meters, most viewers are comfortable with a 65-inch or 75-inch display. A 55-inch can work in smaller rooms, but it may feel small for cinematic content. The old "divide distance by 2.5" rule is a starting point, not a consumer protection law. We suggest using painter's tape on your wall to simulate the footprint before you decide.

### Do I need HDMI 2.1 if I only watch Netflix and YouTube?

No, most streaming apps run fine over HDMI 2.0 because 4K streaming uses lower bandwidth than a game console. HDMI 2.1 becomes relevant for 4K/120 Hz gaming, VRR, ALLM, and eARC audio from a PS5, Xbox Series X, or gaming PC. If you never connect those devices, you can still buy an HDMI 2.1 TV for future-proofing, but it should not be the main reason for an upgrade.

### Which smart TV OS is easier to live with: webOS, Tizen, or Google TV?

Each works well for app access, but they organize menus differently. webOS on LG is quick and favors a pointer-style remote; Tizen on Samsung has a full-screen hub and works well with Samsung phones; Google TV is strongest for Google Assistant and Chromecast users, and it appears on Sony Bravia models. All three run Netflix, YouTube, Disney Plus, and most other major services. Choose the one that fits the voice assistant and phone ecosystem you already use.

### Can QLED match OLED black levels in a dark room?

Not fully. Mini-LED local dimming closes much of the gap, but a bright object next to a dark background can still show a subtle glow, called blooming. OLED controls each pixel individually, so it has no blooming. If you watch a lot of subtitled films in the dark, that difference is easier to see than on a bright sports feed.

## Final verdict: who should buy which TV

| If you... | Choose | Skip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watch movies in a dark room and want four HDMI 2.1 ports | LG OLED evo C5 | Skip if you have no curtains and daytime sun dominates |

The larger cost swings come from screen size, wall-mount install, and extended warranty choices. Before you buy, check the exact HDMI 2.1 port count and smart OS version on your chosen screen size, because specs sometimes shift by region.
