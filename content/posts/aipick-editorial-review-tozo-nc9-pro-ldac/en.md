    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; border-bottom: 2px solid rgba(191, 219, 254, 0.4); padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">
      1. First Impressions and Hardware: 1.47-inch Interactive Display
    </h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      The first thing you notice upon opening the box is the <strong>1.47-inch interactive display</strong> on the front of the charging case. Unlike traditional wireless earbuds that only indicate battery levels through LED flashes or app connectivity, this case allows you to check and adjust settings—such as initial language setup, connection status, battery levels, and EQ presets—directly on the screen. On the unit we received, the time and battery level were displayed clearly, and the screen remained quite legible even when not connected to power.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      However, there is a clear trade-off with this feature. Because the display is designed to run actively, <strong>the case itself consumes more battery than the standard NC9, which lacks a display.</strong> Since TOZO has not publicly disclosed the specific battery capacity of the NC9 Pro case alone, it is difficult to quantify the exact reduction in battery life without rigorous testing. While this is an attractive feature for those who value smart functionality, it is a point to consider for those who prioritize "maximum battery longevity."
    </p>

      <li><strong>Note:</strong> Sensitivity of the touch display and visibility under direct sunlight can vary significantly depending on individual usage environments, so we recommend trying it out in person at a store before purchasing.</li>

    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; border-bottom: 2px solid rgba(191, 219, 254, 0.4); padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">
      2. Sound Verification: LDAC Codec and 10mm SDLC Driver
    </h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      The TOZO NC9 Pro supports <strong>LDAC</strong>, a high-resolution audio codec, and features a 10mm SDLC dynamic driver along with TOZO's OrigX Pro acoustic tuning. The packaging displays Hi-Res Audio Wireless and LDAC certification marks. On supported devices (primarily Android), LDAC allows for higher data transmission compared to standard SBC/AAC codecs, which helps reduce audio loss. However, it is important to note that <strong>iPhones do not support LDAC</strong>; iOS users will connect via the AAC codec, meaning the benefits of LDAC will not be realized.
    </p>
    <ul style="padding-left: 1.5rem; background: rgba(128, 128, 128, 0.06); border: 1px solid rgba(128, 128, 128, 0.2); padding: 1.2rem 1.2rem 1.2rem 2.5rem; border-radius: 8px;">
      <li style="margin-bottom: 0.8rem;"><strong>Resolution:</strong> The muffled sound often found in budget earphones is minimized, and you can noticeably hear more detail in the mid-to-high frequencies, especially when LDAC is active.</li>
      <li><strong>Bass Tuning:</strong> The bass is relatively stable without overly emphasizing punchiness, making it comfortable for long listening sessions with minimal fatigue.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

  <!-- Section 3 -->
  <section class="feature-section" style="margin-bottom: 3rem;">
    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; border-bottom: 2px solid rgba(191, 219, 254, 0.4); padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">
      3. Noise Cancellation (ANC) and Call Quality: Performance by the Numbers
    </h2>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      The TOZO NC9 Pro is equipped with hybrid ANC technology and supports <strong>up to 45dB of noise reduction</strong> according to official TOZO data. A new addition compared to the base model (NC9) is <strong>Adaptive ANC</strong>, which automatically adjusts the noise cancellation intensity based on your surroundings. For call quality, it supports a 6-mic ENC (Environmental Noise Cancellation) system, allowing for relatively clear voice transmission even in noisy environments.
    </p>
    <p style="margin-bottom: 1rem;">
      Keep in mind that the 45dB figure is measured under the manufacturer's specific test conditions (usually based on white noise in a specific frequency band). The actual reduction you experience may vary with broadband or irregular noises like subway sounds or wind. It is best to treat official specifications as "best-case scenario" results.
    </p>
  </section>

    <h2 style="font-size: 1.5rem; font-weight: 700; border-bottom: 2px solid rgba(191, 219, 254, 0.4); padding-bottom: 0.5rem; margin-bottom: 1.2rem;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

    <h3 style="font-size:1.1rem; margin-top:1.4rem;">Can I use LDAC on the TOZO NC9 Pro with an iPhone?</h3>
    <p>No. LDAC is a codec developed by Sony and is not officially supported by iOS. iPhones will automatically connect using the AAC codec, which means you won't be able to experience the high-quality transmission benefits of LDAC.</p>

    <h3 style="font-size:1.1rem; margin-top:1.4rem;">Does the case display significantly reduce battery life?</h3>
    <p>TOZO has not officially disclosed the battery capacity of the NC9 Pro case alone, so exact figures are unknown. However, because the design includes an always-on display, it is highly likely that the total battery life will be slightly lower than the base NC9 (up to 59 hours), which lacks a display.</p>

    <h3 style="font-size:1.1rem; margin-top:1.4rem;">What is the difference between Hybrid ANC and Adaptive ANC?</h3>
    <p>Hybrid ANC is a standard noise-cancellation method that uses microphones both inside and outside the earbuds to cancel out noise. Adaptive ANC is an upgraded feature added to the NC9 Pro that goes a step further by detecting ambient noise in real-time and automatically adjusting the ANC intensity.</p>

    <h3 style="font-size:1.1rem; margin-top:1.4rem;">Should I buy the TOZO NC9 Pro or the base NC9?</h3>
    <p>If you are an Android user who wants LDAC high-quality audio and a smart display UX, the Pro is the way to go. If you prioritize battery life per dollar, the base NC9 is the more rational choice.</p>
