## Editorial Overview

In a closet-sized studio or a galley kitchen with one open counter bay, the usual buying debate is not “which brand” but “which category gets the only free slot.” This head-to-head puts two space-constrained machines on the same table: a 6-place-setting countertop dishwasher and a 0.9 cu ft portable washing machine. Editorial read: both are smaller than a standard kitchen base cabinet and use portable faucet connections, but they solve opposite daily chores. We compare manufacturer-listed depth, width, internal capacity in different units, energy use, water use, and three-year total cost of ownership.

## Introduction: Why the Footprint Fight Matters

Small spaces force cross-category decisions. You may have 18 inches of counter depth and 24 inches of width next to the sink. A compact dishwasher fits there, but so does a portable washer with slight overhang. This comparison helps you decide which tool earns that footprint. We are not picking a “best brand” in a vacuum; we are measuring which machine matches a studio, dorm, or RV galley when closet storage is the limiting factor.

But capacity units differ: dishwashers report place settings, washers report cubic feet, ovens report liters, fridges report cubic feet. So we rely on linear depth, width, and height plus usable interior volume estimates. That is the benchmark lens.

## 1. COMFEE CDC17P1AWW Countertop Dishwasher

**Strengths**
- Fits a 19.6 by 17.2 inch counter patch and sits only 19.7 inches tall, so it works under many wall cabinets.
- Six place settings cover a couple’s dinner or a full day of small dishes; the internal rack layout handles plates, cups, and cutlery better than manual washing in a tiny sink.
- Uses roughly 5–6 liters per cycle on eco mode, which is a fraction of hand-washing water use; no permanent plumbing required.
- Noise listed around 49 dB, quieter than most portable washers.

**Weaknesses**
- Six place settings is small for families; you will run at least one extra cycle after a dinner party.
- It still occupies permanent counter space unless you store it in a closet after each run, which hurts convenience.
- Review concern: public reviews mention that the included quick connector needs careful tightening to avoid minor sink spray drips.
- No cu-ft rating; comparing “liters” requires measuring the rack pocket volume yourself.

**Analysis takeaway**
If your daily friction is dishes and you have exactly one free counter bay, the COMFEE earns the footprint before a portable washer does. Editorial read: this is the stronger category pick when the sink area is already crowded and you cook at home often.

**Verdict**
Buy for studio dish duty if counter space is fixed and you can leave it connected. Skip it if laundry is a weekly crisis and the closet nook is the only spare floor spot.

## 2. BLACK+DECKER BPWM09W Portable Washer

**Strengths**
- 0.9 cu ft drum handles a small load of gym clothes, underwear, and one or two shirts; enough for one person every day.
- A 17.7 by 18.3 inch base footprint and 31.1 inch height fit in many closet storage spaces; casters allow rolling out for laundry day.
- Costs roughly $280–$350, cheaper than in-unit laundry or repeated laundromat trips over one year.
- Simple mechanical and electronic controls, no venting or 240 V circuit required.

**Weaknesses**
- 0.9 cu ft is much smaller than a typical 4.5 cu ft front-loader; sheets and towels strain the drum, and you will use a laundromat for bulky items.
- Height is 31.1 inches, which can collide with a low shelf or hanging rod in a closet.
- Review concern: spin balance can get noisy on wood floors by public ratings; a mat or leveling pad helps.
- Water use per cycle is higher than a countertop dishwasher, though still modest for a laundry task.

**Analysis takeaway**
The Black+Decker is a true closet-first laundry machine, but it is not a full-size replacement. Editorial read: use it as a daily delicates and activewear station, not as a full household laundry system.

**Verdict**
Buy for a small studio or RV with no washer/dryer hookups. Skip it if you want one machine that also dries clothes; in that case look at 24-inch ventless washer-dryer combos, which start around $1,200 and require deeper floor pockets.

## FAQ

### Which machine is better for a rental apartment?
The COMFEE is often easier in a rental because it sits on the counter and uses the kitchen faucet; landlords rarely object to a countertop connection. The Black+Decker is also rental-friendly, but a portable washer may need a bathroom faucet adapter and floor drain access. Check your lease and faucet thread size before buying either.

### Can I put the portable washer in a closet?
Yes, if the closet has at least 18 inches of depth and 32 inches of height clearance. Many users keep a portable washer in a closet and roll it to the bathroom or kitchen on laundry day. The machine should sit on a solid floor, not carpet, to reduce vibration and prevent tipping.

### How do I compare “6 place settings” to “0.9 cu ft”?
They are not directly comparable. Dishwasher capacity is a rack-load count, while washer capacity is drum volume. In tight spaces, compare linear depth, width, and height first, then ask which chore causes more daily pain. That keeps the decision on footprint instead of mixing unit types.

### Will these machines significantly increase my electric bill?
No. A countertop dishwasher uses about 0.6–0.8 kWh per cycle, and a 0.9 cu ft washer uses around 0.3–0.5 kWh. Running each once per day might add $3–8 per month depending on local rates, which is far less than added laundry service or eating out.

### What should I do about the limited capacity?
Treat both as one-person or two-person tools. Run a dishwasher after every meal, not end-of-day, and use the portable washer for small loads daily. A larger 24-inch ventless washer-dryer combo is the upgrade if you need full sheets, towels, and fewer cycles.

## Final Verdict

**Who should buy the COMFEE CDC17P1AWW**
- You cook or eat at home often and the sink is always full.
- You have exactly one available 20-inch-wide counter bay.
- You want a quieter machine and do not want to roll a washer out of a closet.

**Who should skip the COMFEE**
- You rarely hand-wash dishes and already have a dishwasher drawer or kitchen setup.
- Laundry is the bigger weekly logjam in your studio wardrobe.

**Who should buy the BLACK+DECKER BPWM09W**
- You live in a studio, dorm suite, or RV without shared laundry inside the unit.
- You prioritize clean gym clothes and delicates without a 240 V circuit.
- You can store it in a closet and roll it out once a day.

**Who should skip the BLACK+DECKER**
- You need to wash full-size sheets or towels often; a 0.9 cu ft drum will frustrate you.
- You want one machine to dry clothes too; this model washes only.

**Total cost note**
Three-year total cost of ownership for the COMFEE is about $400–$550 including water, detergent, and modest electricity. The Black+Decker runs about $350–$500 over the same period with fewer laundromat trips. Neither is a high-cost appliance, but the bigger savings come from choosing the category that removes your daily space pain.
